How does traveling across oceans for years at a time on a sailboat affect our tax situation?

The CRA(we are both Canadian) states that income tax obligations are based on residency status. "An individual who is resident in Canada during a tax year is subject to Canadian income tax on his or her worldwide income from all sources. Generally, a non-resident individual is only subject to Canadian income tax on income from sources inside Canada." The CRA will consider you a resident of Canada if you have strong residential ties(a house, a spouse or dependents in Canada), they will also look at secondary residential ties like having a bank account, passport, personal property(car), etc. The residence status of a person is typically determined on a case by case basis.

While traveling in the Pacific Ocean between 2016 and 2019, our only residential tie was our bank acounts, and so we only reported income earned from Canadian sources. It would be unusual for a single secondary residential tie with Canada to be sufficient on its own to lead to being classified as a factual resident of Canada while abroad. When living on a boat and sailing within Canada, we are required to pay Canadian tax on all of our income(regardless of where it is earned).

We both work while we travel, earning income from donations as well as the sale of our games and books. Travel outside Canada does not exempt us from filing taxes on a yearly basis.

It is still necessary to have a physical address to file taxes, and so for that we have to rely on the kindness of good friends and family. Most of the sailors we meet do not have this issue because they still have a house and address back at home.
