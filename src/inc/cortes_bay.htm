We had long avoided this bay, namely because it wasn't scenic and because when first entering Desolation Sound both of us prefer to go somewhere nicer. We have skipped it for the past 5 years, but on July 18th we stopped here in hopes of getting a good internet connection before heading back into the land little to no cell service.

The entrance is straightforward, it is necessary to pass south of the red marker, although floatplanes and small roundabouts make frequent trips through here, so watch out. Over the weekend, the floatplane must have come and gone at least 10 times.

The bay is home to both the RVYC and the Seattle YC, both docks were full of giant white icebergs when we arrived. We found a spot near the public dock, just ahead of the mooring field in about 50 ft (at high tide). Most of the boats on moorings occupy the better holding in mud, we had to anchor in rock, but the holding seemed to be adequate. The wind blew out of the northwest, at 15-20 knots for the 3 nights we were there. It got a bit breezy and the sound kept us up, but we didn't budge. This bay has a reputation for having bad holding, but we're not certain if this is because people typically underscope, or because of their anchor, etc... there's no way to know, it may also depend on where you drop the hook, but if it bites down we don't see any reason for concern.

Cell service in here is very good, perfect for us to push project online, send emails, make calls, etc.

People advise against anchoring here in strong southeast winds, but we did not experience any. We did not go to shore, but apparently you can moor your dinghy at the public dock and walk on the road to a few hikes.

Cortes Bay is indeed not scenic, but it's not as bad a stop as we had expected.
