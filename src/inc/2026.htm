In comparison to the past few months spent mostly on our own, we've had a very social August. It seems all our friends made it up north all at the same time. It has been great to run into some familiar faces, meet some new ones, and fill our days wandering in the woods catching up on the time we spent apart. But we really ought to start thinking about returning back south ourselves soon.

Many of our conversations this month have been about languages and coded messages. How to encode a message, how to transmit them if the signal is noisy and how much of this can be done practically with just a few tools. We've added a new page to {Rabbit Waves} talking about just that, covering examples from history and some practical pen-n-paper exercises that can be done by anyone.

Pulling on the thread of auxiliary language, Devine made open call to invite anyone writing Concatenative programs to see if they would be willing to implement a POP-2 compiler for their own language. The idea is to see if this mostly dead language from the 70s could be revived as a language of exchange between the different insular Catlang dialects. Devine has also been busy writing a talk, and making many, many slides for the upcoming Oredev 2026 in Sweden.

This month we participated in Catjam 2026 and released a humble point-and-click called Lepore Express Line. It features many cameos from all our other projects, maybe you can recognize a few? It's written in Uxntal, a concatenative assembly language for a 16-bit ordinator. Have a look at the source. Devine drew the backgrounds, while Rek drew the characters and foreground elements. It's been a while since we've collaborated on an illustration.

Internet on this part of the coast continues to be unreliable, the signal floats in and out of the anchorage, varying in strength, often gone entirely for days. When hoisting the hotspot aloft doesn't work, we stand at the top of mountains in knee-high ferns, laptops in hand, ants crawling up our calves, bald eagles circling above, waiting for the upload to complete. Progress is good, but then it halts at 85%. Come on, you can do it. A mosquito bit me. It's not all for naught, we got the latest weather report on Lynx, responded to a friend's text message, downloaded some emails, pushed changes to a few repositories.

We call them summer projects, because it seems like only the pace of long days without distractions allows for us to dedicate time to them. These projects have very little extrinsic motivation, it's just what we default to when we're left to our own devices. For Rek, it's drawing comics. For Devine, that takes the shape of linguistic experiments, for weeks now Devine has been expanding on the work of Giuseppe Peano who was fascinated by the idea of encoding logic for preservation and communication. Giuseppe dedicated lengths of his life to auxiliary languages to make his mathematical ideas accessible across language barriers; he might have been inspired by today's difficulty of replicating software proofs and crumbling toolchains. Devine has been trying to formalize into BNF what Peano left behind about Latino Sine Flexione, and expanding on his experiments with the axioms of natural numbers.

From across glass rectangles, the homba peer at the tiny ship rocked by a violent wake. The hrududu leaves its suffocating, oily embleer as it sails past Dander and Freckle, who glare back, tharn with terror.

After journeying northward for a long time, they find a quiet bay where the hrududu dare not come and drop a heavy metal hook over the side. They watch the sun set over liquid silver, knowing the elil to be far away and that before them lie the narn days, endless and warm.

They spend delightful hours under the golden radiance of Frith, rowing to the wild woods, wandering its luxurious overgrown trails, occasionally stopping for silflay. When the day wanes, they tell stories under the light of Inlé.

Devine has been haunted by a cursed idea we hinted at back in February. It has come together this month into a notation called Rejoice. We've got a brief cursory description, an implementation and a few examples to demonstrate what it is. It has been fun to puzzle over what it can be used to express.

Relatedly, Giuseppe Peano whose work has directly inspired this project, wrote some of his work in a language he called as Latino Sine Flexione. The idea was to strip Classical Latin down to its simplest possible grammar while keeping its vocabulary recognizable. As a kind of tribute to Peano's work, the Rejoice documentation was translated into that language. Devine tried to emulate Peano's writing style as closely as possible, and .. yeah. Mileage may vary.

By the way, thanks to everyone who reached out with ideas for Rejoice, and helped put it all together, except for that one guy who forwarded us what ChatGPT had to say about it. Don't be that guy.

This is likely very obvious to most people, but recently it dawned on us that clothes are, in a certain way, open-source. We can go to a thrift store, buy a shirt, rip out the seams, study it, lay all of the pieces down over new fabric, trace the patterns, cut them out, and sew it all back together to produce a copy of that same shirt. We've been wanting to learn how to make our own clothes but didn't know where to begin, until this month. Our first project was to reproduce our hats, after the store we bought them at stopped stocking them. We bought black cotton canvas at a local fabric store, took our old hats apart and re-built them(See Rek's hat, and Dev's hat). Later, Devine did the same for our old messenger backpack.
